# Macro Calculator for Weight Loss: The Math Behind Your Plate

Two people can both eat 1,800 calories a day and sit down to completely
different plates. One has a big bowl of rice, lean chicken and fruit. The other
has a couple of eggs, some salmon, a handful of nuts and a lot of empty space.
Same calories, very different amount of food.

A macro calculator explains why. It takes your calorie target and splits it
into grams of protein, carbohydrate and fat. This guide walks through how that
works, step by step, with a real worked example.

## Calories per gram: the number that changes everything

Each macronutrient carries a different amount of energy:

| Macronutrient | Calories per gram |
| --- | --- |
| Protein | ~4 |
| Carbohydrate | ~4 |
| Fat | ~9 |

Fat carries more than twice the calories of protein or carbs per gram. So when
a plan shifts calories toward fat, the total **weight** of food on your plate
drops, even though the calorie total stays the same. That's why a high-fat plan
looks so sparse on paper next to a high-carb one.

## Protein costs the most to digest

Your body burns energy just to digest and process food. This is the **thermic
effect of food (TEF)**, and it isn't the same for every macro:

- **Protein:** roughly 20–30% of its calories are used up in digestion
- **Carbohydrate:** roughly 5–10%
- **Fat:** roughly 0–3%

Protein also keeps you fuller and helps you hold on to muscle while you're in a
calorie deficit. That's why most fat-loss plans set protein first and fill in
the rest afterward. The split you pick affects more than the numbers in your
tracking app.

## Step 1: Estimate your resting burn with Mifflin-St Jeor

The Mifflin-St Jeor equation estimates your **basal metabolic rate (BMR)**, the
calories you'd burn at complete rest. It's one of the most accurate simple
formulas for most adults.

- **Men:** BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) + 5
- **Women:** BMR = (10 × weight in kg) + (6.25 × height in cm) − (5 × age) − 161

*To convert: pounds ÷ 2.2 = kg, inches × 2.54 = cm.*

## Step 2: Multiply by your activity level

BMR only covers rest. To estimate your **total daily energy expenditure
(TDEE)**, multiply it by an activity factor:

| Activity level | Multiplier |
| --- | --- |
| Sedentary (desk job, little exercise) | 1.2 |
| Lightly active (1–3 workouts/week) | 1.375 |
| Moderately active (3–5 workouts/week) | 1.55 |
| Very active (6–7 workouts/week) | 1.725 |
| Extremely active (physical job + training) | 1.9 |

Be honest here. Most people overestimate how active they are, and this
multiplier is where the biggest errors happen.

## Step 3: Set a sensible deficit

For steady fat loss, aim for a deficit of about **15–25% below your TDEE**.
That usually works out to around 0.5–1% of your body weight lost per week.
Bigger deficits make hunger, training quality and muscle retention worse.

## Step 4: Split the calories into macros

1. **Protein first:** about 1.6–2.2 g per kg of body weight (0.7–1 g per lb).
2. **Fat next:** about 20–35% of your calories. Don't go much lower, since fat
   supports hormone health.
3. **Carbs get the rest:** whatever calories are left, divided by 4.

## A worked example

Meet our example client: a 35-year-old woman, 75 kg (165 lb), 168 cm (5'6"),
who trains 3–5 times a week.

**BMR (Mifflin-St Jeor, women):**

(10 × 75) + (6.25 × 168) − (5 × 35) − 161
= 750 + 1,050 − 175 − 161
= **1,464 calories**

**TDEE (moderately active, × 1.55):**

1,464 × 1.55 ≈ **2,269 calories**

**Fat-loss target (about a 20% deficit):**

2,269 × 0.80 ≈ 1,815, rounded to **1,800 calories**

**Macros:**

- **Protein:** 1.8 g × 75 kg = **135 g** → 135 × 4 = 540 calories
- **Fat:** 25% of 1,800 = 450 calories → 450 ÷ 9 = **50 g**
- **Carbs:** 1,800 − 540 − 450 = 810 calories → 810 ÷ 4 ≈ **200 g**

**Result: 135 g protein, 200 g carbs, 50 g fat, about 1,800 calories.**

## Same 1,800 calories, two very different plates

Keep protein at 135 g and change only how the rest is split:

| | Higher-carb plan | Higher-fat plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Protein | 135 g (540 cal) | 135 g (540 cal) |
| Carbs | 225 g (900 cal) | 50 g (200 cal) |
| Fat | 40 g (360 cal) | 118 g (~1,060 cal) |
| **Total calories** | **~1,800** | **~1,800** |
| **Total macro grams** | **400 g** | **303 g** |

The calories are the same, but the higher-carb plan gives you about 100 more
grams of food. Add the water and fiber in fruit, vegetables, potatoes and rice,
and the difference you see on the plate is even bigger. Neither plan is wrong.
The best one is the one you can stick to, and that fuels your training.

## The calculator gives you a starting point. Tracking tells you the truth.

Every formula is an estimate. Mifflin-St Jeor is usually within about 10% for
most people, but you're not "most people". You're you. So:

1. **Track your intake and weigh yourself daily for two weeks.** Same time each
   morning, after using the bathroom.
2. **Compare weekly averages**, not single days. Water, salt and hormones make
   daily weigh-ins bounce around.
3. **Adjust from real data:**
   - Losing about 0.5–1% of body weight per week? Stay the course.
   - Not moving after two full weeks? Cut 100–200 calories, mostly from carbs or fat.
   - Losing much faster, or feeling drained in training? Add 100–200 calories back.

Keep protein steady through every adjustment.

## Quick recap

- Protein and carbs are ~4 calories per gram. Fat is ~9.
- Protein has the highest thermic effect and protects muscle, so set it first.
- Mifflin-St Jeor × activity factor = your estimated maintenance calories.
- Take 15–25% off for fat loss, then split into grams.
- Track for two weeks and let the trend guide your next move.

*These numbers are general guidance for healthy adults, not medical advice. If
you're pregnant, have a medical condition, or have a history of disordered
eating, work with a qualified professional before changing your diet.*
